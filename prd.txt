Product Requirements Document (PRD): XRPL Institutional Tokenized Fund Management Protocol
Executive Summary
This Product Requirements Document defines the architecture, features, and specifications for an agentic, non-simplified, institutional-grade fund management protocol built exclusively on the XRP Ledger (XRPL). This protocol enables multi-asset tokenization, robust compliance controls, fund management features across jurisdictions, and advanced DeFi capabilities, leveraging the latest XRPL primitives: Multi-Purpose Tokens (MPT/XLS-33), Permissioned Domains (XLS-80), Decentralized Identity (DID/XLS-40), and Native Lending Protocols (XLS-65/66).

1. Purpose and Scope
The protocol will enable:

Tokenization of any asset type (securities, RWAs, funds, exotics) as fully programmable, compliant blockchain tokens.

Layered compliance, identity, access controls, and reporting for institutions across multiple jurisdictions.

Decentralized, upgradeable fund operations: issuance, transfers, subscriptions/redemptions, asset management, lending, trading, governance, and audits.

No vendor lock-in, full ledger-side validation, and deep agentic prevention of semantic simplification or hallucination: each primitive is implemented and validated according to latest XRPL specifications.

2. Functional Architecture
2.1 XRPL Ledger Primitives
Tokenization Engine:
Uses MPT (Multi-Purpose Token, XLS-33/XLS-89) standard for all issued shares, underlying assets, fund units, and derivatives. Supports:

On-chain metadata (1024-byte URI, immutable)

Configurable fixed/elastic supply

Transfer fee collection

Authorized holders (issuer-approved)

Global and per-account lockback/clawback options

Asset fractionalization, non-transferable features as required (credits, locked shares)

Permissioned Domains (XLS-80):
Implements programmable KYC/AML and institutional gating. Domains restrict access to DEXes, lending pools, governance, and fund actions:

Credential-gated membership (via issued KYC credentials)

Rule-based access control objects (Domain Owners, Domain Rules)

Domain deletion/updating is gated to owner account; each change on-chain

Privacy-preserving verification; no sensitive data on-chain

Domain linking with Permissioned DEX or Lending objects

Decentralized Identity (DID/XLS-40):

Each protocol user must anchor a DID object per XRPL standards

DIDs link bidirectionally to credential documents (W3C standards)

DIDs persist across jurisdictions for universal portability

Identity cannot be forged: mutual on-chain and off-chain referencing

Credentials can be issued, revoked, verified for any fund action

2.2 Fund Management Workflow
2.2.1 Fund Creation and Asset Tokenization
Setup:

Deploy Fund smart contract (via Hooks or native account logic) with issuance rules, manager, and parameters

Issue primary share tokens (MPT) with full on-chain and off-chain metadata (ISIN, prospectus hashes, custodial info)

Issue all underlying/constituent assets as separate MPT tokens or fractionalized MPT batches

Types Supported:

Securities (bonds, equities, bills, cash instruments, mutual funds, ETFs)

Real-world assets (real estate, commodities, artwork, infrastructure)

Synthetic instruments (indexes, derivatives, custom baskets)

Credit exposures (yield funds, lending pools as tokens)

Hybrid products (multi-asset overlays)

2.2.2 Subscription, Redemption, Transfers
All actions are permissioned; only domain-credentialed accounts can interact

Subscriptions/Redemptions:

User submits request (on-chain), linker contracts instruct mint/burn of share tokens, all flows governed by MPT rules

Each event creates audit trail with signed ledger references

Transfers:

On-chain transfer requires credential check (domain membership), off-ledger transfer may trigger KYC event

Transfer fees credited per MPT settings, clawback initiatable by issuer if regulatory breach detected

Reporting/Auditing:

All ledger events are anchored to fund audit contracts (Hooks with custom memos)

Real-time reports for NAV, AUM, compliance breaches, investor lists

2.2.3 Trading and Asset Allocation
Native DEX:

MPT tokens trade directly on XRPL DEX, supporting AMM liquidity pools

Permissioned DEX: Orders only fillable by credentialed, whitelisted accounts; all order matching linked to compliance hooks

Automated Market-Making:

AMM liquidity provisioning configurable per fund for basket and share tokens

Integration with external oracles (DIA/Band) for asset pricing

2.2.4 Lending and DeFi
Native Lending Protocol (XLS-65/66):

Fund can participate in or provide pooled lending

Depositors and borrowers credential-gated

Lending objects fully on-chain, first-loss, interest computation, repayments, defaults, capital protection

2.3 Compliance, Identity & Governance
2.3.1 Credential Issuance & Verification
Credentials issued/accepted for KYC, AML, FATF, and jurisdictional approval per domain/fund

Each credential linked to DID and verified as part of transaction permissioning

Credential revocation and renewal is on-chain, creating immutable compliance logs

2.3.2 International Compliance Matrix
Fund supports multiple regulatory templates:

MAS (Singapore), FINMA (Switzerland), ESMA/MiCA (EU), VARA/ADGM (UAE), SFC (Hong Kong), SEC (USA)

Permissioned Domains linked to jurisdiction-specific rules; cross-domain operations logged and auditable

Transfer, custody, and reporting rules per asset and investor type

2.3.3 Governance & Upgrades
All protocol parameters managed via governance contract

Admin actions (pausing, upgrading, asset swapping, parameter tuning) require multisig of Fund Managers + compliance agent

Hooks implement instant upgrade propagation or staged multi-signature rollouts

3. Technical Specifications
3.1 Ledger and Protocol Components
XRPL version >= 2.6.0 with all amendments enabled: XLS-33 (MPT), XLS-80 (Permissioned Domain), XLS-40 (DID), XLS-65/66 (Lending)

Programming: Use xrpl.js TypeScript/Javascript SDK; Hooks written in assembly-permitted Wasm or Rust

APIs: Full support for HTTP/JSON-RPC and WebSocket API version 2

Custody: Multi-signature with HSM integrations for institutional-grade key management

Auditability: Each ledger event logs audit hash, memo field and links to off-chain reporting systems

3.2 Security & Risk Protocols
Key Management:

All manager/issuer keys in HSM/MPC or equivalents; rotation on event of compromise

Transaction Safety:

Compliance checks before every mint/burn/transfer/trade; denial on credential mismatch

All Hooks subjected to formal verification and code audits pre-deployment

Data Privacy:

Use privacy-preserving metadata for identity and credential linking; no PII on-chain

4. Integration & Interoperability
4.1 External Protocol Connectivity
Oracles: DIA, Band, Chainlink nodes for asset pricing feeds

Bridges: Axelar bridge for EVM asset representations (if allowed by fund scope)

Custodians: API connections to enterprise-grade custodians (Ripple Custody, Fireblocks, etc.), with multi-sig and compliance audits

4.2 Modular Extension
System must allow relayer contracts or sidecar modules for basket composition, performance monitoring, investor registry, tax reporting, etc.

All features upgradeable via on-chain governance and protocol hooks

5. User Interfaces & Developer APIs
5.1 APIs
Javascript/TypeScript SDKs: Full reference implementation, with sample code for token mint/burn, credential issuance/verification, DID registration, permissioned domain management, lending pool operations

WebSocket, REST, and CLI interfaces: Must expose all transaction types, fund events, reporting, and compliance logs

Monitoring Dashboards: On-chain explorer for fund events, investor registry, and audit trails

5.2 UX Requirements
All administrative actions require stepwise confirmations and multi-factor authentication

Investors and managers must have interfaces for credential status, trading, lending pool data, subscription/redemption workflow, compliance status

6. Testing, Validation & Maintenance
Reference Suite: Automated and manual test suites for all ledger events, Hooks, credential objects, transfer events, and permissioned domain logic.

Upgrade and Downtime Management: Blue-green deployments for contract upgrades; failed amendments must not halt fund operation.

Documentation: Fully versioned, non-simplified, protocol documentation for all primitives, workflows, and edge cases.

7. Regulatory, Legal, and Audit
Immutable Audit Logging: All compliance actions, admin changes, and fund transactions must log on-chain audit hashes, linking to off-chain reports

Regulatory Templates: Protocol parameters deployable per-jurisdiction, parameters switchable/upgradable

8. Example Implementation Scenarios
Tokenized Money Market Fund:
Fund contract deploys as MPT; only KYC'd investors can subscribe. Redemptions, fee collection, and clawbacks governed by compliance hooks.

Tokenized Real Estate Fund:
Assets represented as MPTs; transfers only allowed for credentialed and jurisdiction-assigned DIDs. AMM enables fraction trading.

Structured Credit/Hybrid Fund:
Asset baskets constructed via relayer contracts; investor yield computed based on holding time, weighted via time-based Hooks.

Closing Statement
This PRD ensures agentic, non-trivial, and unsimplified implementation. All primitives, compliance flows, asset management, governance, security, and reporting must be coded and validated against the latest XRPL amendments and specification; hallucination or simplification is non-compliant. No logic, workflow, or feature may be omitted or replaced with “best practices” or high-level wrappers—every component must be implemented as per canonical XRPL standards and linked regtech/identity amendments.
